Theory: What Is a For-and-Against Essay?

⏱ 3 minutes

It is discursive writing: you discuss the advantages and disadvantages of a specific topic. Click each part of the structure!

1 · Introduction

Present the topic. Make a general remark about it — without giving your opinion.

2 · Arguments FOR

Points in favour, each supported by justifications / examples.

3 · Arguments AGAINST

Points against, also with justifications / examples, in a separate paragraph.

4 · Conclusion

Summarise pros and cons and give a balanced consideration: In my opinion, I believe, I think…

🎩 Style: keep it formal!

For-and-against essays are written in a formal style. Avoid…

❌ Personal language — I know, I am sure

❌ Contractions — I’ve got, don’t

❌ Colloquial expressions / idioms — What’s up?

✅ Clear topic sentences

✅ Linkers to organise points

✅ Examples / justifications for every argument

The Internet as a homework tool — its advantages and disadvantages.

3. What style should you use?

A formal style — no personal language, contractions or idioms.

4. What points should you include?

Points for and points against, each with justifications / examples.

Model Essay & the Table

⏱ 5 minutes

Read the model essay. The linkers are colour-coded — notice how they guide the reader!

Para 1 · IntroIn our modern, high-tech society and with 24-hour access to the Internet, it’s no surprise that children are using homework-help websites more and more. However, there are arguments for and against using the Internet as a homework tool.

Para 2 · ForThere is no doubt that using the Internet as a homework tool has its advantages. First of all, it is a quick way to find information. This means that students have more free time for other activities. Secondly, the Internet provides a variety of sources. For example, students can read lots of information and form their own opinions by comparing and contrasting ideas.

Para 3 · AgainstHowever, there are drawbacks to using the Internet as a homework tool. One drawback is that students don’t use their writing skills. For example, they just copy the information which is not always well-written. Another disadvantage is that some websites not only contain poorly written material, but also inaccurate information. As a result, some students may include a lot of facts in their work which are incorrect.

Para 4 · ConclusionAll in all, I think that the use of the Internet as a homework tool is a welcome and useful development. Unfortunately, though, it is not free from risks and we must bear those risks in mind if we want students to get the most from the Internet.
